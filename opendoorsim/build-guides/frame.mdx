This guide walks you through constructing the freestanding wooden door frame that forms the physical backbone of the OpenDoorsim rig. By the end, you will have a sturdy, level frame with a fully hung door panel that swings, latches, and behaves like a real interior door — ready for sensor mounting and simulation use.

## Materials Needed

This phase uses the structural lumber, plywood base, and hardware listed in the [OpenDoorsim Bill of Materials](/opendoorsim/bill-of-materials). Before starting, confirm you have the following on hand:

- 3/4" plywood sheet (cut to 4'×4' for the base plate)
- Two 2×4 studs at 84" (vertical uprights)
- One 2×4 stud at 36" (horizontal header)
- One interior door panel (standard 80"×36")
- Three door hinges with mounting screws
- Door latch set with strike plate
- 3" wood screws
- Diagonal cross-brace lumber (2×4, approx. 48")
- Drill, circular saw, level, tape measure, and square

## Build Sequence

<Steps>
  <Step title="Cut the Base Plate">
    Cut a sheet of 3/4" plywood down to a **4'×4' square**. This forms the weighted foundation that keeps the entire assembly freestanding. Once cut, mark a clear **center line** running the full 4' length — this will serve as your alignment reference for positioning the uprights.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cut and Assemble the Frame Uprights">
    Using a circular saw, cut your lumber to the following dimensions:

    - **2× vertical uprights** — 2×4 at **84"** (7 feet)
    - **1× horizontal header** — 2×4 at **36"**

    Stand the two uprights parallel and attach the header across the top, flush with the outer edges, to form a rectangular door opening. Fasten with 3" screws through the header into the top of each upright, driving at least two screws per joint.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Attach Frame to Base">
    Position the assembled frame on the base plate, centered on the marked center line, with the uprights sitting flat on the plywood. Drive **3" screws** down through the base of each upright into the plywood — a minimum of three screws per upright in a triangular pattern. Check that the frame stands vertically plumb in both directions before fully tightening.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Mount Door Hinges">
    Mark hinge locations on both the **door frame upright** and the **door panel edge**:

    - Top hinge: 7" down from the top of the door
    - Bottom hinge: 11" up from the bottom of the door
    - Middle hinge: centered between the two

    Chisel or route shallow mortises at each mark so the hinges sit flush, then fasten each hinge leaf with the provided screws.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Hang the Door">
    With a helper holding the door panel in position, align the hinge leaves on the door with those on the frame upright and seat the hinge pins. Open and close the door slowly to **check swing clearance** — there should be approximately 1/8" clearance at the top and latch-side edge. Adjust hinge mortise depth if binding occurs.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Install Door Latch">
    Bore the latch bore hole in the door edge and the cross-bore through the door face per the latch manufacturer's template. Insert the latch mechanism and secure the faceplates. With the door closed, mark the strike plate position on the opposite upright, chisel a mortise, and fasten the strike plate. **Test the latching action** several times — the latch bolt should engage and release smoothly without force.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Brace the Assembly">
    Cut a 2×4 diagonal cross-brace to fit between two corners of the base plate on the non-hinge side. Fasten it flat on the base with 3" screws at each end. This triangulates the base and prevents racking or tipping during simulation use. Give the completed assembly a firm push from multiple angles to confirm it is stable.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tips

<Tip>
  **Pre-drill all screw holes** before driving fasteners — 2×4 lumber will split, especially near the ends, if screws are driven without a pilot hole. Use a drill bit approximately 1/8" smaller than your screw shank diameter. Additionally, **use a level at every stage** when installing the uprights: a frame that is even slightly out of plumb will cause the door to swing open or closed on its own.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  A fully assembled door frame is tall and top-heavy. **If the frame tips over, it can cause serious injury** and damage sensors or the door panel. Do not skip the diagonal cross-brace step, and never leave the rig unattended in an environment where it could be bumped or knocked. Ensure the base is fully secured before hanging the door or beginning any sensor installation work.
</Warning>
